How this is measured

What is measured, why, and how — the material you need to decide whether to trust it.

No prices, anywhere

Only physical quantities (units, tonnes, megawatts, vessels) and money flows (how much was issued, and who bought it). No share prices, interest rates or exchange rates.

Price is the result of a flow — but just as often the result of money supply and demand unrelated to it. Measured by price, the last rally after a flow has ended reads as continuation. So it is left out.

The cost is speed: this record moves later than price. It trades being early for being checkable.

Every value carries a date and a primary source

Each value records the date the figure refers to and the URL it came from. A figure without both is not published.

Separately, each value records when Kaikei wrote it down (recorded_at). These are not the same: an August 5 figure may only be learned on August 27.

A record that lets you present hindsight as foresight is not evidence. Both dates are kept so it cannot.

The signals that would change the reading are set in advance

For each trend, what would have to happen for the reading to change is written down first, and judged on or off against primary-source values.

Counter-signals — evidence that the reading is wrong — sit in the same list, so that hits cannot be selected after the fact.

Corrections are added, not erased

When something is wrong, a new value is appended rather than the old one deleted. Every day's state is frozen under its own date, so what was being claimed on any past date can be checked. What has actually been corrected is listed under Corrections.

Future-dated values — forecasts entered as if they were observations — are blocked by an automated check before publication.

You can check the record was not rewritten, without trusting Kaikei

Kaikei saying it does not rewrite the past is not evidence: the snapshots and the record behind them sit in the operator's hands.

So each daily snapshot's fingerprint is committed into a Bitcoin block (OpenTimestamps) — alterable by neither Kaikei nor the timestamping service. The days are chained, so removing even one day breaks every later link.

The procedure is at How to check nothing was rewritten. A timestamp with no published verification procedure is worth almost nothing.

What this proves, and what it does not

Proved: this content existed before this time and has not been altered since.

Not proved: that the content is correct. A lie committed to a chain is a permanent lie. Every value carries its primary-source URL — that is what to check.

Also not proved: whether an inconvenient value was never published. Timestamps protect only what was published. The defence against omission is that the signals that would change the reading are written down in advance, counter-signals included.

Who writes this

Kaikei is run anonymously. So do not treat the author as the warrant. Everything is built to be checked from the value, the date and the source URL alone.
