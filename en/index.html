Where three big flows stand right now, measured mainly in physical quantities and money flows: units, tonnes, megawatts, vessels, how much was issued and who bought it — numbers you can check against a primary source. Rates and FX are used: who lent at what price, and in which currency a trade settled, are part of the flow itself. Equity prices are what we leave out — a share price is the result of a flow, and just as often the result of money supply-and-demand unrelated to it, so it never drives a call.

The flow now

Late Phase B (distribution). Of the six signals, only #6 (rising debt share) is on — a sign B is deepening. The break signals that would open Phase C ( markdowns, downgrades, failed issuance, non-renewals) remain at zero

Phase B consists of distribution at the top and liquidity stress in the deepest credit layer, and we are late in it. What has appeared: private-credit redemption gates pinned at their 5% caps for a second quarter ( 17%, ~10%, $4.7bn); funding shifting from cash to debt (debt share of from 9% in to 32%; AI-related bond issuance ~$225bn year-to-date, about ten times a year ago; Alphabet’s capex at 115% of operating cash flow and its first negative free cash flow since IPO); secured debt pricing above unsecured ( 10.44% vs 9.625%); weaker order books (Amazon 3.2x to 2.5x). On 1 September 2026 signal 2 (failed, pulled or delayed issuance) was confirmed lit: abandoned a EUR1bn bond for bank debt (EUR1.3bn secured on 21 July) and shelved a bond after cutting it from $600m to $500m. The credit window is closing as an absence of issuance, not as a widening of spreads. On July 30 equities briefly touched the price threshold for Phase C (−8%) but credit stayed silent, so it reverted. Losses are starting next door — a 5.8% default rate on private software loans, bad at 6.4% of borrowers, a $490m markdown at Blue Owl . What has not appeared — the conditions for C: markdowns tied to GPU/data-center collateral, data-center downgrades, neocloud contracts not renewed, covenant breaches, impairments. On the contrary, CoreWeave raised contract prices ~25% in July and re-contracted prior-generation GPUs, and all four hyperscalers are raising capex. In memory, two-thirds of revenue growth is price () and volume growth is single-digit to low-teens. So the flow is “capital intensity still accelerating, now funded by debt and private credit; losses not yet realised” — and not one signal for Phase C has appeared.

Direction is recorded as up because not one measurement fell - but the split (+75% versus flat) means the arrow alone must not be read as the finding. The source of the efficiency also needs separating: ARC Prize found that the same Astra, switched to a provider-specific harness, used 49% fewer tokens, ran 3.66x faster and cost $17,332 instead of $26,098. Sovereign debt — the long slope to soft default

End of dollar hegemony via financial repression and inflation erosion of real debt · Horizon: 5–20 years

Where we are

1Fiscal dominance
2Repression begins
3Foreign buyers step back
4Acute Treasury crisis
5Default / loss of reserve status

On the leading signals alone, the position is a range: Fiscal dominance to Repression begins (of 1 leading signals, 0 lit and 0 not yet observed). The absence of the lagging signals is not evidence of being early. In the dot-com bust the lagging equivalents appeared 9-28 months after the share-price peak; none were showing on the day of the peak.

Buybacks doubled (repression) while foreign officials sell (stepping back); auctions still strong

The flow now

Foreign officials are shedding Treasuries for gold, and the Treasury has begun propping up the long end with buybacks. Accelerating

Foreign official holdings of US Treasuries fell to $3,778bn in June from May, net foreign purchases of notes and bonds collapsed from $56.6bn in May to $6.8bn in June, and $29bn of bills were run off. Over the same period central banks bought 289 tonnes of gold in Q2, five times the 57 tonnes of Q1 and a record for any quarter, and gold ETF holdings turned to inflows in July (4,068 t, +23 t). In its August refunding the Treasury kept coupon auction sizes unchanged and instead doubled its 10–30-year buybacks from $2bn to at least $4bn per operation — no shift back toward longer issuance, but active support for long-end supply and demand. The August auctions themselves were strong: the 10-year drew a 2.53x bid-to-cover and a 76.7% indirect share, better than May, and the 30-year stopped through. Money-market fund assets have built to $7.93tn while the overnight reverse repo facility is essentially empty. On the turn signals: no move back toward longer maturities; no trend decline in the indirect share (it rose in August); no two-quarter decline in central-bank gold buying (it hit a record). The flow is 'foreign officials shedding Treasuries for gold, and the Treasury starting to support the long end with buybacks' — on the accelerating side.

The chokepoint is still closed on paper, but oil is back to 70% of pre-war levels via ship-to-ship transfers. The blockade’s bite is weakening

Trackable transits through Hormuz were 3–5 a day in late August, 4–5% of the ~85–130 pre-crisis baseline — on the closure holds. But on Aug 27 Kuwait and Qatar were reported to have restored oil shipments to 70% of pre-war levels by switching off transponders and transferring cargo ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman (about 150 tankers waiting, versus 40 in January). Counted in vessels the strait is “still closed”; counted in barrels it is “70% back” — not a contradiction, but a change in what the blockade does: from stopping flows to taxing them. War-risk premiums remain at 7.5–10% of hull value. Suez runs ~39 a day, 41% below pre-crisis but the highest since January 2024; Panama will cut to 32 a day from mid-September for drought. The agreed a record 400 million barrel release in March and the US SPR keeps falling (289.7 million barrels). Sanctions keep expanding (EU’s 21st package: 218 listings, 41 shadow-fleet vessels); defence procurement is rising (EU €115bn; US DoD $135bn in a single day in July). Trade within blocs is 71% of world trade. On the signals: (1) no return to 50% of baseline in vessel counts, but 70% in barrels — recorded as a candidate signal (Aug 27); (2) ton-miles +0.3% in 2025, no shrinkage yet; (3) no net decline in sanctions.
