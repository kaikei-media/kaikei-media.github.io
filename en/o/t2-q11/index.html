This is the shape of a dead end: Hormuz was risky so the bypass was used; the bypass route through the Red Sea was itself attacked and thinned; then the pipeline was hit, and the fallback became the chokepoint that was risky in the first place. Note a discrepancy in the shutdown date between reports of 11 and 13 September; both are kept.
2026-09-11Saudi Arabia's 1,200km East-West pipeline from Abqaiq to Yanbu was shut on 11 September 2026 after drones launched from Iraq's Maysan province struck it near Riyadh and Medina. The energy ministry called the shutdown precautionary; the foreign ministry confirmed the damage and no group claimed responsibility. It had been carrying 4-5 million barrels a day, 4-5% of world supply, against a design capacity of about 7 million and a pre-crisis average of 2.8 million.Al Jazeera（サウジ外務省・エネルギー省の発表を報じたもの）

Why we watch this

Until 2026-09-13 every geopolitical measurement here was global, not Japanese: vessel counts and ship-to-ship transfers describe world flows, not what reaches Japan. This item pairs (1) the share of Japan's crude that transits Hormuz with (2) how much the bypass routes actually carry. If the bypass narrows, the 93% has nowhere to go.
