Glossary

Plain-language explanations of the technical terms used here. In the text, the first occurrence of each term is underlined with dots — tap it for the explanation.

AAA bond rating symbol — the second-highest band.
ABSAsset-backed security — a bond backed by the cash flows of specific assets.
AISThe Automatic Identification System — ships broadcast their position; switching it off is itself a signal.
AIラボThe companies that build AI models themselves — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and peers.
ASPAverage selling price — revenue divided by units.
BACICEPII's product-level bilateral trade dataset, reconciled where exporter and importer figures disagree.
Bad PIKPIK (payment in kind) that reflects distress — interest added to principal because the borrower cannot pay cash.
BBA rating symbol for how likely a bond is to be repaid. The ladder runs AAA, AA, A, BBB, then BB. BBB and above is 'investment grade'; BB and below is seen as materially riskier.
BBBA bond rating symbol: the lowest rung of 'investment grade'. One notch below it, some investors are no longer allowed to hold the bond.
BCREDBlackstone Private Credit Fund — a very large private-credit vehicle whose redemption behaviour is treated as a bellwether.
BDCBusiness development company — a listed vehicle that packages private credit for retail investors.
BIMCOAn international shipping industry association that writes standard contract forms and publishes cargo volume statistics and outlooks.
Blue OwlA US private-credit manager; markdowns in its funds signal losses starting to surface.
book-to-billThe ratio of new orders to revenue. Above 1 means the order book is growing.
capexCapital expenditure — money spent on long-lived assets like factories and data centres. The cash goes out first; the return comes over years.
CapexCapital expenditure — money spent on long-lived assets. Cash out first, returns later.
CEPIIA French international economics research institute; it produces the BACI dataset of product-level trade between countries.
CFCash flow — actual cash in and out, as opposed to accounting profit.
CliffwaterA US asset manager whose retail private-credit funds are watched as a window into redemption pressure.
CLOA bond built by pooling many corporate loans and slicing the repayments into ranks. Upper ranks are paid first; lower ranks absorb losses first.
COMEXA US commodities exchange reporting metals futures and warehouse stocks.
ConstellationA US power company with a large nuclear fleet, signing long-term supply deals with data centres.
CoreWeaveA company that buys GPUs at scale and rents compute. It is heavily debt-financed, so its loan covenants are closely watched.
CoreweaveA company that buys GPUs at scale and rents compute, financed largely by debt.
DCShort for data centre (as in DC-ABS, bonds backed by data-centre assets).
DC-ABSBonds backed by data centres — the building and the rent its tenants pay serve as collateral. If the tenant stops paying, the data centre itself is sold.
DDoSA denial-of-service attack that floods a service with traffic.
DDTLA delayed-draw term loan — a committed facility drawn down in stages, with conditions attached.
DSCRDebt service coverage ratio — earnings divided by debt payments. Loan covenants often set a minimum.
FCFFree cash flow — operating cash minus capital expenditure. Negative FCF means capex is being funded by borrowing.
FIMAThe Fed's repo facility for foreign official institutions — dollars against Treasuries, without selling them.
FitchOne of the major credit rating agencies.
FY24Fiscal year 2024. A company's financial year does not always start in January.
GPU担保Borrowing money with AI chips (GPUs) themselves pledged as collateral. Chips are replaced by newer models within a few years, so how fast that collateral loses value is hard to predict.
GWGigawatt — 1,000 MW. Roughly the output of one large nuclear reactor.
IEAThe International Energy Agency, formed by major consuming countries; it publishes inventory, supply and demand statistics and outlooks for oil and power.
IMFThe International Monetary Fund — an organisation funded by member countries that lends to states unable to meet payments and publishes regular global economic outlooks.
JICPAThe Japanese Institute of Certified Public Accountants, the body of accountants who examine financial statements and issue audit guidance.
KplerA firm that tracks vessels and cargoes to estimate physical commodity flows.
LMEThe London Metal Exchange — trades industrial metals and reports warehouse stocks.
MMFMoney market fund — a near-cash fund investing in short-term government paper.
MWMegawatt — a unit of electrical capacity. Data-centre size is measured in MW.
NAVNet asset value — a fund's assets minus liabilities, per unit.
NGDSThe multi-year framework under which the US military contracts commercial carriers for parcel delivery.
OTFA Blue Owl fund focused on lending to technology companies.
PIKPayment in kind — interest paid by increasing the principal instead of paying cash.
PJMThe largest US grid operator; its interconnection queue delays feed straight into data-centre timelines.
PLAThe People's Liberation Army of China.
PortWatchAn IMF dataset of port calls that estimates cargo flows per port from ships' broadcast position signals.
PPAPower purchase agreement — a long-term contract to buy electricity, signed before a data centre is built.
QEQuantitative easing — a central bank buying bonds to inject money and hold rates down.
QRAThe US Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement.
RPORemaining performance obligations — contracted but not yet recognised revenue.
SanDiskA memory maker whose disclosure split revenue growth into price and volume, giving a read on real demand.
SHFEThe Shanghai Futures Exchange, which reports Chinese metal stocks.
SOFRThe main US overnight rate, based on repo transactions; floating-rate loans are priced over it.
STSShip-to-ship transfer at sea.
take-or-payTake-or-pay — the buyer pays the agreed amount whether or not it takes delivery.
TICUS Treasury International Capital data — monthly statistics on foreign holdings of US securities.
TrendForceA Taiwanese research firm publishing semiconductor price and shipment forecasts.
VortexaAnother vessel-tracking firm estimating physical oil flows.
WTOThe World Trade Organization, which sets the rules for trade between countries and handles disputes.
YTMYield to maturity — the annualised return if a bond is held to maturity.
オフバランスKeeping debt or assets off a company's own balance sheet — for example by routing them through a separate entity or a lease. The obligation to pay still exists; it just is not shown.
ガイダンスA company's own forecast for its next period's revenue or profit. Share prices tend to move when it is missed.
クレジットLending, and the question of whether the borrower can repay. It refers to the world of loans and bonds rather than shares.
グリーンボンドA bond issued on the condition that the money raised is spent on environment-related projects. The restricted use attracts a different set of buyers.
コベナンツCovenants — conditions attached to a loan; breaching them can trigger repayment.
コンバインドサイクルA power plant that burns gas to generate electricity, then reuses the waste heat to generate more. More output per unit of fuel, but it takes years to build.
シンジケーションSyndication — distributing a large loan among lenders.
スプレッドSpread — the extra yield demanded over government bonds.
ソフトデフォルトSoft default — eroding the real value of debt through inflation and financial repression rather than failing to pay.
チョークポイントA chokepoint — a narrow passage whose blockage halts the whole flow.
テナントThe party renting a building. In the data-centre context, the company that rents the facility and puts its machines in it.
デフォルトFailure to repay borrowed money on the promised date. It can be triggered by a late interest payment alone.
デフォルト率Default rate — the share of borrowers failing to pay.
トリプルネットリースA lease where the tenant pays taxes, insurance and maintenance on top of rent. The landlord's income is predictable, but the hole is bigger if the tenant fails.
トンマイルTonne-miles — cargo weight times distance. Rerouting raises it even when volumes are unchanged.
ネオクラウドCompanies that buy GPUs in bulk and rent compute to others (e.g. CoreWeave).
ノヴォロシースクA Russian port on the Black Sea and a main loading point for crude oil and grain; a stoppage there shows up directly in export volumes.
ハイパースケーラThe largest operators of their own data centres — usually Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta.
バイバックA company buying back its own shares. Fewer shares outstanding means a larger claim per share — but the cash spent does not go into equipment.
ビット出荷Bit shipments — memory volume measured in bits rather than dollars.
プロダクトタンカーA ship that carries refined fuels such as petrol and diesel rather than crude oil. Its routes and freight rates move separately from crude tankers.
ベンダーファイナンスVendor financing — the seller lends to or invests in the buyer so the buyer can purchase its products.
リバースレポA transaction in which the central bank temporarily takes in cash from financial institutions. The amount is used as a gauge of how much money is sloshing around the market.
レポA repo — short-term borrowing secured by bonds.
上場来Since the company's listing. 'First since listing' means it has not happened since the IPO.
保税Bonded — inventory held before customs duty is paid; often outside headline stock figures.
借入比率The share of capital spending funded by new borrowing rather than internal cash.
償却Depreciation — spreading an asset's cost over its useful life.
入札An auction in which buyers bid for newly issued securities.
劣後Subordinated — the tranche that absorbs losses first in exchange for a higher yield.
受注倍率Orders received in a period divided by revenue recognised in the same period. Above 1 means the order book is building faster than sales are being delivered.
含み損An unrealised loss — not yet recognised because the asset has not been sold.
営業CFCash actually received from the core business over a year, minus cash paid out. Unlike profit, it counts only money that really passed through the company's hands.
変電所A substation — converts transmission voltage into usable power.
契約非更新A contract not being renewed at expiry — one of the first places real demand shows up.
定例発行計画The quarterly refunding plan — how much government debt is issued at which maturities.
年初来Year to date — from 1 January to today.
年限構成The maturity mix of government debt issuance — how much is short-dated versus long-dated.
影の船団The shadow fleet — ageing tankers with opaque ownership and insurance used to move sanctioned oil.
循環取引A sale where the seller funds the buyer, who then uses that money to buy the seller's product. Revenue appears, but no new money has come in from outside.
応札倍率Bid-to-cover ratio — total bids divided by the amount offered. Lower means weaker demand.
担保付Secured — debt backed by specific collateral, normally cheaper than unsecured debt.
昇圧Stepping voltage up so power can travel long distances.
格付A credit rating — an agency's assessment of repayment likelihood.
流動性ストレスLiquidity stress — money cannot be withdrawn quickly, even though it is not yet lost. It shows up as redemption gates and margin calls, before losses are realised.
減損An impairment — writing down an asset whose value is judged unrecoverable.
瀬取りShip-to-ship transfer at sea — used to move sanctioned cargo, decoupling paperwork from physical flows.
無担保Unsecured — debt with no specific collateral, normally more expensive than secured debt. An inversion suggests the collateral itself is doubted.
短期国庫証券Government IOUs repaid within a year. Because the wait is short, they are widely used as a place to park cash.
私募クレジットPrivate credit — lending by funds outside banks and public bond markets; it is not marked daily, so losses surface late.
系統接続Grid interconnection — hooking a facility up to the transmission network. Queues here delay projects even when construction is done.
耐用年数Useful life — the number of years over which an asset is depreciated.
解約ゲートA redemption gate — a cap on how much investors can withdraw in a period. Hitting the cap means more want out than the cap allows.
設備投資Spending on long-lived assets such as plants and data centres.
買い戻しBuybacks — the government repurchasing its own outstanding bonds.
起債Issuing bonds to raise debt.
金融抑圧Financial repression — holding interest rates artificially low so the real burden of government debt falls.
間接落札Indirect bidders — mostly foreign official buyers bidding through dealers.
需要倍率The ratio of orders to the amount offered in a bond sale. A falling ratio means weaker demand.
